# Give A Wonderful Day — Funding and Strategic Partnership Brief

> **Working brief — not an offer to sell securities, a solicitation of investments, or a claim that contributions are tax-deductible.** The project has not provided verified historical financials, funding commitments, valuation, securities terms, or tax-exempt determination. Accordingly, this brief presents a mission and decision framework, not a priced financing proposal.

## Opportunity Statement

Give A Wonderful Day is building a mission-led digital experience that makes small acts of encouragement visible, repeatable, and easier for community partners to organize. The immediate product is a mobile demo: people create a brief kindness moment, receive a transparent simulated record, and explore aggregated impact. The long-term question is not whether to add Web3 features, but whether decentralized records can provide a safer, more useful form of provenance than conventional systems.

| Audience | Value proposition | Appropriate first engagement |
|---|---|---|
| **Community and nonprofit partners** | A low-friction experience for organizing encouragement-oriented moments without exposing sensitive stories. | Design-partner discovery and a tightly scoped pilot. |
| **Corporate social-impact teams** | A potential employee-engagement or sponsored-moment program with transparent aggregate reporting. | Sponsored pilot discussion, subject to legal and brand review. |
| **Foundations and grantmakers** | A testable approach to community connection, product learning, and responsible digital-impact infrastructure. | Restricted pilot or capacity-building grant inquiry. |
| **Values-aligned donors** | Support for mission delivery, governance, safeguards, and measurement rather than speculative digital assets. | Major-gift or fiscal-sponsor conversation once compliance readiness is verified. |
| **Technology collaborators** | A clear research question around privacy-preserving provenance, security, and user benefit. | Pro bono review, research collaboration, or scoped build support. |

## Funding Architecture

The recommended early-stage posture is **philanthropic and partnership-led**, rather than an equity or token raise. A proposed charitable organization should not try to combine public-benefit language, donation appeals, speculative token economics, and product experimentation in one public launch. If a separate commercial technology entity is ever pursued, it should be legally distinct, with a written relationship to the mission organization and independent advice on securities, tax, governance, and intellectual-property issues.

| Funding lane | What it can responsibly fund | Preconditions | What it must not be presented as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundational grants** | Pilot design, safeguards, evaluation, community partnership, and non-sensitive product research. | Clear charitable purpose, eligible applicant/fiscal sponsor, and approved budget. | A guaranteed or unrestricted source of operating funds. |
| **Major gifts and recurring giving** | Mission delivery, care programs, and approved capacity needs. | Gift acceptance process, compliant solicitations, tax-status disclosure, and donor communications. | Tax-deductible unless that status is confirmed. |
| **Corporate sponsorship** | Sponsored program cohorts, employee participation, or underwritten product access. | Sponsor policy, mission alignment review, recognition terms, and participant safeguards. | An endorsement that compromises program independence. |
| **Program service revenue** | Optional partner-facing implementation, reporting, or white-glove program support. | Legal review, cost allocation, mission alignment, and privacy controls. | A substitute for the organization’s charitable purpose. |
| **In-kind and pro bono support** | Security audit, design research, hosting credits, or independent evaluation. | Written scope, conflict review, and security/privacy acceptance criteria. | A reason to bypass quality or compliance review. |

## Sustainable Monetization Policy

The mobile experience should remain free to an individual creating or receiving a kindness moment during the demo and early pilot. Monetization, if adopted, should flow from **institutional value creation** rather than from participants’ emotional data or speculative assets.

| Potential mechanism | Pilot-stage recommendation | Mission and risk controls |
|---|---|---|
| **Partner implementation fee** | Consider only after an approved pilot shows repeatable partner value. | Publish a scope, prohibit sale of participant data, and separate program reporting from identifiable stories. |
| **Sponsored moment cohort** | Explore with one mission-aligned sponsor per pilot. | Sponsor cannot choose or exploit vulnerable recipients; recognition is secondary to participant dignity. |
| **Optional premium reporting** | Evaluate after data governance is proven. | Aggregate and de-identify reporting; never monetize recipient profiles or private messages. |
| **Merchandise or symbolic digital keepsakes** | Defer until legal, tax, consumer-protection, and security review. | No investment framing, financial return, transferability promise, or forced wallet setup. |
| **DAO or token mechanics** | Do not include in the first funding model. | Require an explicit mission-benefit thesis, specialized counsel, security review, and governance analysis before reconsideration. |

## Board-Ready Use-of-Funds Framework

No funding target or allocation amount is stated because none was supplied or validated. Before asking any funder for a specific amount, the board should approve a bottom-up pilot budget that identifies the number of partner cohorts, estimated direct-program cost, product and security scope, legal/compliance work, evaluation, and contingency. The fundraising request should match a named milestone and show what will be learned if the milestone succeeds or fails.

| Budget category | Decision question for the board | Evidence expected before funding request |
|---|---|---|
| **Program delivery** | What specific participant experience will be funded? | Partner scope, safeguarding plan, and per-cohort assumptions. |
| **Product and security** | What is essential to test the pilot safely? | Requirements document, threat model, build versus buy decision, and independent review plan. |
| **Compliance and governance** | What legal and operational work must precede a public launch? | Entity/status checklist, policy register, and counsel workplan. |
| **Measurement and learning** | What will show whether the mission hypothesis is working? | Evaluation questions, minimum data set, retention/deletion schedule, and reporting template. |
| **Operations** | Who owns partner support, incidents, and financial controls? | RACI, escalation path, and monthly financial cadence. |
| **Contingency** | What risks could delay the pilot or require a pause? | Explicit risk register and reserve policy approved by the board. |

## Partner Due Diligence Questions

Potential funders and partners should receive clear answers to the following questions before committing. The project should answer “not yet established” plainly where evidence does not exist, rather than using aspirational claims.

| Area | Question | Current status based on repository evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Mission** | Which population and moment of support is the first pilot designed to serve? | Requires a named pilot thesis and partner selection. |
| **Governance** | Who has fiduciary authority and how are conflicts managed? | Requires formation and board documentation. |
| **Financial controls** | How are gifts, restrictions, expenses, and reserves governed? | Requires a treasury and gift-acceptance policy. |
| **Impact** | What outcome is measured beyond app activity? | Requires an evaluation design before a pilot. |
| **Privacy and safeguarding** | How are recipient stories and sensitive data protected? | The demo avoids collection; production policy remains required. |
| **Web3 utility** | Why is a ledger needed and what harm does it avoid? | Not yet established; this is a later research decision. |
| **Technical assurance** | Who reviews the code and any contract before funds are at risk? | Requires security scope and independent review. |

## Capital-Raising Readiness Checklist

The project should not issue tokens, solicit investments, or publish valuation language as a proxy for early traction. For a proposed nonprofit, the first funding ask should be a clear charitable or programmatic ask made only after the appropriate organizational, tax-status, and solicitation reviews. If the project later considers a separately incorporated commercial technology company or a crypto-enabled offering, it must conduct an independent legal and financial review. The SEC’s current educational material underscores that crypto assets can include digital securities and that the rights associated with a crypto asset are material to its regulatory analysis.[1]
